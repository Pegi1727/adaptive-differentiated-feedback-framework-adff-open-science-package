## 📖 About the Project

This repository contains the open-science package for the study:

> **Adaptive Differentiated Feedback Framework (ADFF): Enhancing EFL Writing Development through Proficiency-Sensitive Generative AI Feedback**

The project introduces the **Adaptive Differentiated Feedback Framework (ADFF)**, a proficiency-sensitive generative AI feedback model designed to align the complexity, explicitness, and pedagogical support of automated feedback with learners’ English proficiency levels.

The framework was developed for English as a Foreign Language (EFL) writing contexts and was evaluated across different proficiency levels, including **A2, B1, and C1 learners**.

---

## 🎯 Research Aim

The study examined whether proficiency-sensitive generative AI feedback could enhance EFL learners’ writing development more effectively than generic, non-differentiated AI feedback.

The ADFF framework aims to:

- Adapt feedback to learners’ proficiency levels
- Calibrate the complexity of explanations
- Adjust the explicitness of corrective feedback
- Provide developmentally appropriate metalinguistic support
- Promote learner engagement with feedback
- Support more effective revision and writing development

---

## 🧪 Research Design

The study employed a quasi-experimental mixed-methods design to compare:

1. **Adaptive differentiated AI feedback**
2. **Generic non-differentiated AI feedback**

Learners’ writing performance was evaluated across proficiency groups and assessment stages.

The research package includes the manuscript, figures, data resources, analysis materials, and supporting documentation.

---

## 📊 Key Results

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| Main effect of feedback condition | Significant |
| Feedback method × proficiency level interaction | *F*(2, 117) = 18.42, *p* < .001, ηp² = .18 |
| Largest post-test improvement | Observed among A2 learners |
| General finding | ADFF outperformed generic AI feedback in supporting EFL writing development |

The statistically significant interaction suggests that the effectiveness of AI-generated feedback was influenced by learners’ proficiency level. The findings therefore support the value of **proficiency-sensitive feedback design** rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.
